LTGDV vs LTC Explained

LTGDV (loan to gross development value) and LTC (loan to cost) both cap senior debt, and the lower of the two sets the facility. The senior caps lenders publish run 65 to 70 percent LTGDV and 80 to 90 percent LTC: Shawbrook and West One both state 65 percent LTGDV and 85 percent LTC. When cost is low relative to GDV, LTC binds first; when cost is high relative to GDV, LTGDV binds first.

Worked example at 65 percent LTGDV and 85 percent LTC

GDV 2.5m, total costs (excluding land) 1.6m, land bid 0.4m, so total project cost 2.0m. Caps as Shawbrook and West One publish them.

  • Senior at 65 percent LTGDV: 2.5m x 0.65 = 1.625m maximum.
  • Senior at 85 percent LTC: 2.0m x 0.85 = 1.7m maximum.
  • LTGDV binds first, so the facility sizes to 1.625m.
  • LTC binds first only when total cost is below 65 / 85 = 76.5 percent of GDV.

Equity gap

Equity gap = total project cost minus senior debt. In the worked example: 2.0m minus 1.625m equals 0.375m. That has to come from developer equity, stretch senior, mezzanine or pref-equity.

  • Hampshire Trust Bank

  • United Trust Bank
    Up to 65% LTGDV and 90% LTC (Sept 2025 product guide); its web page says up to 70% LTGDV

  • Aldermore
    Up to 70% LTGDV / 85% LTC; bespoke pricing
    property development finance page
  • Maslow Capital
    Up to 70% of GDV and 90% of cost, for larger schemes; margins from 3.8%
    development finance page

Each lender's own website or product guide, read 24 November 2026. Terms change often and depend on the scheme and the borrower; a lender's maximum is not an offer.
