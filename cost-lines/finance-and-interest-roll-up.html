Finance Interest Roll-Up

Finance interest rolls up on a drawdown profile (an S-curve for new build). The workbench assumes the average outstanding balance over the build is 50 percent of peak debt. Fees come on top: the development, bridging and exit lenders we checked that publish an arrangement fee all charge 2 percent, and exit fees range from none (Precise, MT Finance) to a lender-specific charge.

S-curve assumption

New-build drawdown follows an S-curve: slow start, rapid mid-build draws, slow tail. The workbench interest line assumes an average outstanding balance of 50 percent of peak debt over the build period; replace it with your lender's drawdown schedule when you have one.

All-in fees

  • Arrangement: 2 percent at Precise, LendInvest, Together, MT Finance, Shawbrook, Allica, Hampshire Trust Bank and Castle Trust (their own guides, November 2026).
  • Exit: none at Precise or MT Finance; Hope Capital charges 0.5 percent on heavy refurbishment only. Development lenders set exit fees per deal.
  • Monitoring surveyor: charged per site visit on development loans; ask for the lender's schedule.
  • Broker fee (if used): agreed with the broker and disclosed in the offer.

IRR vs flat rate

A 9.5 percent rate is not a 9.5 percent cost. Add a 2 percent arrangement fee and a 1 percent exit fee to a 12-month loan drawn in full and the cost is 9.5 + 2 + 1 = 12.5 percent of the facility for the year. Repay in 6 months and the same 3 percent of fees is spread over half a year, so the annualised cost is 9.5 + 3 / 0.5 = 15.5 percent. Compare lenders on the all-in cost for your expected hold.
