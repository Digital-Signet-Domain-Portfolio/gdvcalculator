What the BCIS General Building Cost Index measures

The BCIS General Building Cost Index, published by BCIS (part of RICS), tracks the change over time in a contractor's input costs: nationally agreed labour rates, materials prices and plant costs, across all types of building work. It is a cost index, so it does not by itself give a £/sqm rate and it does not necessarily follow a contractor's actual site costs. It is distinct from a tender price index (such as the BCIS All-in Tender Price Index), which tracks the prices contractors actually tender and moves with market conditions rather than input costs.

Methodology

BCIS aggregates tender prices from a large UK sample, weighted by project size and type. Rates are tender-priced (not actual completion). Inflate between cuts using ONS construction output inflation as the working escalator.

Sense-checks before relying on a rate

  • Building height: high-rise (>6 storeys) adds 10 to 20 percent to BCIS general.
  • Spec: passivhaus or low-carbon adds 15 to 25 percent.
  • Brownfield: contaminated remediation can add £200 to £500/sqm depending on profile.
  • Inflation: between BCIS cuts add ONS construction-output inflation pro-rata.

BCIS build cost FAQ

What is the BCIS General Building Cost Index?

The BCIS General Building Cost Index (GBCI) is a cost index published by BCIS, part of RICS. It measures the change over time in a contractor's input costs - nationally agreed labour rates, materials prices and plant costs - across all types of building work. It is a cost index, not a price: it does not itself give a £/sqm rate and it does not necessarily track a contractor's actual site costs. Its job is to update or escalate build-cost figures between dates.

Is the General Building Cost Index the same as a tender price index?

No. The General Building Cost Index tracks input costs (labour, materials, plant). A tender price index - such as the BCIS All-in Tender Price Index - tracks the prices contractors actually tender, which move with market conditions, workload and margin, and can rise or fall independently of input costs. CIL indexation, for example, uses the RICS CIL Index (which BCIS calculates from its All-in Tender Price Index) for planning permissions granted on or after 1 January 2020, not the General Building Cost Index.

Does 'North West = 100' mean the BCIS General Building Cost Index?

No. That figure is a regional location factor, not the time-based cost index. To compare build cost between regions we rebase our indicative £/sqm bands so the North West sits at 100; other regions adjust either side (Outer London around 119, North East around 91). It is a spatial adjustment and is separate from the General Building Cost Index, which measures cost movement over time.

What is the current BCIS index value for 2026?

BCIS publishes the live index behind a RICS subscription, so we do not republish a specific 2026 index number here.
